1. Project Overview

VishvKisan is a web-based agricultural marketplace prototype intended to connect farmers with customers and bring selected agricultural information and tools into one digital experience. It focuses on market access, price awareness, farming information, government-scheme awareness, community learning and responsible pesticide-use education.

1.1 Problem statement

  • Farmer income and market access: intermediaries and limited direct-buyer access can reduce the share farmers receive; farmers may sell at low prices while consumers pay substantially more.
  • Price transparency: farmers may lack timely, comparable market-price information for informed selling decisions.
  • Government schemes: information about schemes, eligibility, subsidies and applications can be fragmented or difficult to access.
  • Post-harvest losses: gaps in collection, storage, cold-chain access, packaging and transport can damage produce or limit market reach.
  • Digital and knowledge barriers: language, digital literacy and fragmented information can make online services difficult to use.
  • Consumer transparency and safety: buyers may want clearer information about produce origin, quality and responsible pesticide practices.

1.2 Objectives

  • Provide a digital place to showcase produce and explore customer demand.
  • Make displayed prices and simple profit estimates easier to compare.
  • Bring crop, weather, farming, scheme and pesticide-safety information together.
  • Design for rural users and future regional-language accessibility.
  • Define a pathway from prototype to a measured Assam pilot.
Scope and limitations: The current deliverable is a demonstration prototype. Production-grade authentication, persistent database, live market/weather feeds, payments, official farmer verification, fulfilment logistics and government/private partnerships must be confirmed, implemented and tested before being described as operational.

3.2 Future production architecture

A production version would separate the web client, API/application services, persistent database, third-party integrations and operational administration. Access should be role-based, sensitive data minimised, and integration failures handled safely.

4. User Journeys and Data Flow

4.1 Farmer journey

  • Open the platform and choose the farmer workspace.
  • Explore tools and review displayed information.
  • Create or update a produce listing where implemented.
  • Review price information and use the profit calculator if available.
  • Manage listing/order details and provide feedback according to current prototype behaviour.

4.2 Customer journey

  • Open the customer marketplace.
  • Browse/search produce and review product information.
  • Compare displayed prices and product details.
  • Use the available order action; call it a demonstration flow if no live order backend exists.
  • Provide feedback on usability, trust and product-information clarity.

4.3 Proposed farm-to-customer logistics

Proposed sequence: farmer/FPO supply → collection and aggregation → quality check → storage/cold chain where needed → sorting and packing → local delivery partner or pickup → customer. Each stage requires partners, service standards, cost estimates and responsibility allocation.

4.4 Data-flow considerations

  • Product listings should include only necessary information.
  • External price comparisons should identify source, unit, location and date.
  • Order records must distinguish simulated orders from confirmed real transactions.
  • Weather, scheme and crop information should include source and freshness checks where possible.
  • Administrative changes to verified records should be auditable in a production system.

5.1 Responsible pesticide-use information

The platform can share awareness about label directions, appropriate use, training, integrated pest management concepts and harvest waiting periods using authoritative agricultural guidance. Farmer identity verification alone does not prove produce is pesticide-free or residue-free; such claims require defined testing and verification.

5.2 Testing checklist

  • Confirm pages load and navigation works.
  • Test desktop/mobile layouts, listings, search, filters, price displays and product details.
  • Test forms, validation, empty states, error messages and calculations.
  • Verify external links and data freshness where integrations exist.
  • Clearly label demo orders and sample prices.
  • Test access control and privacy before collecting real user data.
  • Validate pesticide-safety content against authoritative agricultural guidance.

6. Proposed Public–Private Partnership (PPP) Model

This is a proposed operating arrangement, not evidence of an approved government agreement. It requires partner selection, legal review, funding approval, procurement/compliance review and a signed agreement.

6.1 Proposed roles

ParticipantProposed responsibilities
Government of Assam / authoritiesPolicy/institutional support; scheme coordination; oversight; possible authorised verification support; proposed pilot funding subject to approval.
Private technical / operating partnerPlatform development and maintenance; technical integrations; daily operations; user support and improvements.
Farmers / FPOsList produce, participate, provide feedback and follow applicable safety/trading requirements.
ConsumersBrowse produce, compare information, use order flows and provide feedback.

6.2 Proposed financial assumptions

ItemProposal
Pilot geographyTwo to three Assam districts; expansion only after evaluation.
Indicative funding₹1–2 crore proposed; not an approved allocation or confirmed budget.
Profit / operations splitProposal states 60% of profit to government and 40% for operational costs—not 60% to the private partner.
Contract conditionsDefine revenue, expenses, net profit, audit rules, timing, losses, ownership, data governance and termination terms through legal review.

6.3 Main risks and mitigations

RiskProposed mitigation
Slow adoption / low trustVillage demonstrations, simple/local-language interface, field agents and clear verification.
Fund misuse / poor governanceAudits, quarterly reporting, milestone-linked funding and transparent procurement.
Overlap with ONDC, e-NAM or mandisPosition as complementary where appropriate; assess interoperability later.
Storage/transport gapsStart in selected districts using existing FPOs, collection centres and local transport.
Low revenueConservative assumptions, staged investment and single-district fallback.
Privacy / securityMinimise data, restrict access and review applicable data-protection duties.
Seasonal volatilityDiversify produce and provide clearly sourced price information; avoid fixed-price promises.

7.2 Intended impact

VishvKisan aims to improve market access, agricultural information access, scheme awareness, responsible farming and consumer transparency. Increased income, lower consumer prices, reduced losses and food-safety improvement are hypotheses to measure during a properly designed pilot, not results already achieved.
